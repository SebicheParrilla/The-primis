# Glossary


Link back to the main [README](README.md)!

| **electropneumatic** | Electropneumatics is the integration of electrical control and pneumatic systems to automate mechanical movements using compressed air and electrical signals.

| **Drivetrain** | The mechanical system responsible for transmitting power to the robot's wheels. |

| **Vision-Based Navigation** | Navigation that uses camera data and image processing to determine the robot's position and required movement. |

| **Pneumatic Engine** | The compressed-air-powered engine used as the primary mechanical power source of AIZA.|

| **Mechanical power** | Mechanical power is the rate at which mechanical work is done, typically expressed as force multiplied by velocity for linear motion or torque multiplied by angular velocity for rotational motion, and measured in watts.

| **Air Supply and Management System** | The system responsible for storing, regulating, and delivering compressed air to the pneumatic engine. |

| **eCVT** | Electronic Continuously Variable Transmission. A transmission system that electronically changes the effective drivetrain ratio instead of using fixed gears. |

| **Regulates** | In this context, to regulate means to automatically reduce and maintain the downstream air pressure at 30 psi, even though the upstream supply is 100 psi.

| **IMU** | Inertial Measurement Unit. A sensor that measures motion and orientation using accelerometers, gyroscopes, and other sensing elements. |

| **Encoders** | A sensor used to measure rotational movement and determine the speed or position of a rotating component. |

| **Crackshaft** | A rotating mass connected to the pneumatic engine that helps stabilize its rotational motion and provides the input to the transmission. |

| **Flywheel** | A rotating mass connected to the pneumatic engine that helps stabilize its rotational motion and provides the input to the transmission. |

| **Inertia** | Rotational inertia, also known as moment of inertia, measures an object's resistance to changes in its rotational motion around a given axis.

 | **Photointeruptor** | A Photointerrupter is a transmission-type photosensor, which typically consists of a light emitting elements and light receiving elements aligned facing each other in a single package, that works by detecting light blockage when a target object comes between both elements, acting as an optical switch.

 | **Displacement** | Volumetric displacement in the dictionary is the volume of air per revolution that passes through a mechanical pump when the pressure at the intake and the exhaust is the same as that of the atmosphere Also called: swept volume.

| **Psi** | Is a unit of pressure defined as one pound‑force applied to an area of one square inch

| **Burst pressure** | Burst pressure is the maximum pressure that a component, vessel, or sensor element can withstand before it fails or ruptures.

| **Gear ratio** | A gear ratio is the relationship between the rotational speeds or number of teeth of two meshed gears, typically expressed as the ratio of the driver gear's speed or teeth count to that of the driven gear.

| **Torque** | A force that tends to cause rotation.

| **Rotational speed** | A gear system consisting of a sun gear, planet gears, and ring gear used to provide different transmission ratios. |

| **Horsepower** | Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred, and horsepower is a non‑SI unit of power originally defined by James Watt as 33,000 foot‑pounds of work per minute.

| **Ah** | Is a unit of electric charge defined as the quantity of electricity transferred when a steady current of one ampere flows for one hour 

| **Solenoid** | An electrically controlled valve used to control the flow of compressed air to the pneumatic engine. |

| **UART** | Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter, the serial communication interface used between the Raspberry Pi and Cytron Motion 2350. |

| **Planetary Transmition** | A gear system consisting of a sun gear, planet gears, and ring gear used to provide different transmission ratios. |

| **HSV** | Hue, Saturation, and Value. A color representation used by the vision system to separate colored course markings from the environment. |
