# Pneumatic Engine

The pneumatic engine is the primary mechanical power source of AIZA.
It converts the energy stored in compressed air into rotational motion,
which is then transferred to the transmission.

The engine consists of four cylinders, and 2 crankshafts, two pistons per crankshaft. Compressed air is admitted to each cylinder at a specified point in the crankshaft's rotation, producing a sequence of piston movements
that maintains continuous rotation.

In this case, if we were to look at the engine from the front, (facing the black gear), the engine will spin clockwise.The hose outlined in purple is the supply of the switch, and the blue hose is the one activated and pressurised by the switch, if you follow the line you'll see that the line  top hole of the piston is the one being pressurised so the piston will expand, making the crankshaft spin 90°.

**90° - 180°**
</td>
<td width="60%" valign="top">



The piston has expanded to it's maximum, and now needs to contract to keep spining the engine, but now the pushrod sets the valve to the middle which means neither of the outputs of the valve are being pressurised.

This causes the engine to lock itself, since the piston can't make the valve give itself pressure to contract and keep the chain reaction going. 

To counter this, one must install a flywheel specially on 1 cylinder engines so that the inertia from the flywheel keeps the motor spinning. Another way to fix this is to add more pistons, if you have a piston that is in a different timing, it can push another piston out of it's lock zone and vise-versa.

</td>
</tr>
